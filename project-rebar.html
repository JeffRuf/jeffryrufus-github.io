Rebar-tying robot

A gantry robot that rides on a rebar grid and ties the crossings. I designed the chassis in SolidWorks, made the BOM, built the prototype, integrated the hardware and tested it. The hardware is still in development at the company, so I can't share photos.

Sizing the chassis

  • The 427 × 427 mm footprint came from the rebar grid spacing and the tool loads. The frame uses 2020 aluminum extrusions, drivetrain mounts and a guide-rail-stabilized linear actuator for the tying tool.
  • The first frame used heavy 4045 extrusions. I went back to the loads and did the hand calcs: stiffness was the real limit, not strength.
  • I moved to 2020 and doubled only the guide-rail members. The robot got 25% lighter while beam deflection stayed under 0.5 mm with a safety factor above 2 under a 2× dynamic load.

Building it

  • One fastener size across the build and standard extrusion hardware, so assembly is quick. Gusset plates resist frame racking.
  • I made the BOM and built the prototype myself.

Testing it

  • Paired 30 Hz visual servoing with a 200 Hz PID loop. Tool repeatability stayed under 5 mm over 100+ runs.
  • Rebar-crossing detection went from under 50% to 90% after I labeled 200+ crossings the robot missed in live grid runs and retrained YOLOv5.
